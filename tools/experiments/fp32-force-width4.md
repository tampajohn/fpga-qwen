The candidate-only ThinLTO `-force-vector-width=4` experiment is rejected before scored timing. The patch applies to base `9d1e62a`; it adds an experimental probe-builder option only. Do not apply it to production or describe it as an exact optimization.

Diagnostic relinking of the validated `--unroll-count1` artifact produced function-attributed LLVM optimization records. In the FP32 `evalNba` function, a loop selected vectorization width2/interleave1; other loops were judged not beneficial or blocked by unidentified array bounds. Source line locations were unavailable, so the records do not conclusively identify which source loop each decision refers to. The filtered records are `reports/fp32-b8-loop-vectorization.txt`. The Darwin linker options used were `-Wl,-mllvm,-lto-pass-remarks-output=vectorization.yaml`, with `-pass-remarks`, `-pass-remarks-missed` and `-pass-remarks-analysis` set to `loop-vectorize` through the same mechanism.

Forcing width4 produced `fmul.4s`/`fadd.4s`, but also strided gathers, stack spills and horizontal reductions in the hot function. Those instructions alone do not prove a rounding-order violation. Numerical testing supplied the decisive evidence: all nine tiny exact-rational fixtures (workers1/3/8, rows1/3/7, widths8/16/24) passed, yet full Q/gate/down outputs differed from the validated strict FP32 artifact for both1 and8 workers. Q had14789 differing outputs, gate22023 and down7693; maximum absolute differences ranged2.74e-6–3.76e-6.

The required same-arithmetic output contract therefore failed. No tolerance was relaxed, no performance run followed, and no general compiler-bug claim is made. The complete correctness result, binary hashes and candidate build manifest are in `reports/fp32-b8-force-width4-rejected.json`. The active probe helper was restored to the validated unroll-count1 implementation. Its measured gains remain valid without forced vector width.
